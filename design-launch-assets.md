# Ship by Sunday Design Launch Assets

Last updated: 2026-03-07

## Goal

Define only the design assets that matter for day-one revenue, trust, and course usability.

Ignore brand-filler work. Ignore the full Instagram grid. Ignore broad social packaging.

## Priority tiers

- `must-have` = needed to support conversion, checkout confidence, or student navigation at launch
- `should-have` = useful if capacity exists after the must-have list is done
- `later` = explicitly not required for day-one launch

## Must-have assets

### Asset name

`Founder proof strip`

### Purpose

Turn the abstract credibility section into clear, visual proof that the founder ships.

### Placement / channel

- sales page proof section
- checkout-supporting proof area if needed

### Copy needed

- `10 years in product, from design to engineering`
- `Scaled Delivery Dudes to $73M GMV and a $23M acquisition`
- `Built GPTs for Devs to 100K users in its first week`
- `Taught 110+ engineers through Vibe with AI`

### Visual direction

- modular editorial cards
- strong black / white contrast
- one metric or proof statement per card
- minimal decoration, heavy legibility

### Priority

`must-have`

## Production order

2. Checkout assurance row
3. 4-block artifact graphic
4. Worksheet bundle preview
5. Start Here roadmap graphic

## Inputs still needed

- final approved proof statements
- final guarantee wording
- final lesson naming conventions
- actual worksheet exports or screenshots

## Definition of done

The launch design layer is done when:

- the sales page has the proof visuals it needs
- checkout has enough visual trust support
- onboarding has a clear roadmap graphic
- the course library has a minimal but coherent cover system
- YouTube has one usable thumbnail direction for the first launch video
