Ship by Sunday is not another prompt pack, another course, or another generic starter template. It is a blueprint-first shipping system for people who can get software moving, but keep losing the thread between the idea, the prompt, the code, and the launch.

The product does one simple job: it turns what you are trying to build into durable context. That context becomes a Ship by Sunday Blueprint, a set of agent-ready packs, and a starter path shaped around the product instead of around a blank template.

## It starts by locking the product truth

Most AI builds do not fail because the model is too slow. They fail because the product truth is vague. The user, problem, promise, scope, design direction, pricing logic, and setup constraints live in scattered chats and half-decisions.

Ship by Sunday starts by pulling those decisions into one place. The blueprint flow asks for the product definition first, then keeps narrowing until the build has a clear target. The point is not to plan forever. The point is to stop re-explaining the same product every time you open a new tool.

## It turns that truth into agent-ready packs

Once the product truth is stable, Ship by Sunday compiles it into files different tools can actually use. The Design Pack carries the visual direction. The Marketing Pack carries the public story. The Build Pack carries the implementation intent. The Prompt Pack gives AI tools the same scope boundary instead of a stale summary.

Those packs are the important part. They make the context reviewable, reusable, and portable across sessions. A builder can hand the same product truth to Codex, Claude, ChatGPT, a designer, or their own future self without restarting from a blank chat.

The goal is not to create a prettier planning document. The goal is to create the handoff the implementation can trust. When the product truth is file-shaped, every later change has something concrete to compare against.

That is what makes the system different from a prompt library. The prompts are downstream of the product contract, not the source of truth.

</BlogSplitSection>

## It gives the build a repo-shaped path

Ship by Sunday also points the work at a starter system instead of leaving the builder with only prose. The starter path is meant to carry real product surfaces: auth, billing, docs, protected workspace structure, public marketing surfaces, and the seams where agents should keep working.

That does not mean every project becomes the same app. It means the first build starts from a durable shape. The product truth, the public copy, the route map, and the starter assumptions all come from the same contract.

A weekend build is useful only if it still makes sense on Monday. That is why the system cares about contracts, routes, generated files, public metadata, and the difference between source truth and generated output.

The builder should not have to rebuild context from memory every time they ask for another feature. The repo should carry enough truth that the next change starts from the product instead of from vibes.

</BlogSplitSection>

## The goal is to keep shipping after the weekend

The promise is not that one prompt magically creates a company. The promise is that a founder or technical builder can move from idea to a reviewable product system without scattering the decisions that matter.

By the end, Ship by Sunday should give you three things: a clear product truth, agent-ready packs, and a starter path that can keep absorbing future changes. That is the thing it does. It makes AI-assisted building less like improvising in a chat window and more like improving a product that already has a spine.
