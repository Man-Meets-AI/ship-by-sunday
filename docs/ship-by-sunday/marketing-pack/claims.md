# Ship by Sunday Claims System

Use this file to keep marketing outputs grounded in claims the product can actually support.

## 1. Evergreen approved claims

These can be reused across homepage, email, social, and internal briefs without needing fresh verification, as long as the product has not materially changed.

- Ship by Sunday is a blueprint-first system, not a course-first wrapper.
- Ship by Sunday helps builders ship a real, durable product by Sunday.
- The product captures product truth first, then compiles agent-ready packs.
- The core blueprint outputs are Design, Marketing, Build, and Prompt Packs.
- The starter system is meant to keep shipping after the weekend, not just create a one-night demo.
- The product is best for scoped web apps, MVPs, narrow tools, and practical products.
- The product is not meant for giant multi-system startup ideas.
- The guarantee is about clarity and tighter scope, not a promise that Ship by Sunday builds the product for the buyer.

## 2. Claims that require current-fact verification

These may be used only if they are re-verified from current product configuration, current blueprint facts, or a fresh approved source.

- live price or price display
- live guarantee window or refund mechanics
- founder shipping stats such as commits, launches, revenue, or growth figures
- current launch timing
- current checkout behavior details
- any statement that depends on the present feature set, integrations, or availability

## 3. Claims that are allowed only when customer-specific facts exist

These are valid shapes of claims, but must be derived from the customer's own blueprint facts rather than reused blindly.

- primary user and problem framing
- promised user outcome
- design-direction or visual-thesis statements
- channel-specific promises
- product-specific workflow or operational claims

## 4. Do-not-say rules

Do not publish copy that:

- says we will ship the product for the buyer
- implies success is automatic just because AI is involved
- positions the product as a generic “build anything” starter
- reintroduces old CEO / Design / Build / Ship framing when it conflicts with the blueprint-first product
- invents testimonials, proof, performance claims, or customer outcomes
- promises support, services, or access paths that do not actually exist
- treats current facts as evergreen truth

## 5. Required caveats when relevant

- If a claim depends on current facts, say that it was verified or route it through the current-facts workflow first.
- If the output is strategy or ideation rather than final copy, label it as draft material for review.
- If the copy mentions guarantees, keep the exact guarantee framing aligned with the current approved offer.
