# Landing Page Rules

Use this sheet for landing pages and branded marketing pages.

Marketing pages usually follow these rules too unless a task-specific plan says otherwise.

## First-screen rules

- The first viewport must read as one composition, not a dashboard.
- The brand or product name must be a hero-level signal.
- Keep the hero budget tight: brand, one headline, one short supporting sentence, one CTA group, and one dominant image.
- Do not place stats, schedules, metadata bars, promos, or other secondary content in the hero unless the brief explicitly requires it.
- Do not place detached labels, badges, promo stickers, or callout boxes on top of hero media.
- Keep the text column narrow and anchored to a calm area of the image.

## Composition

- Prefer a full-bleed hero or another dominant visual plane.
- Default to no cards in the hero.
- Use cards only after the first screen and only when they improve comprehension.
- Make the headline readable in one glance on mobile.
- Let spacing, scale, and cropping do the heavy lifting before adding chrome.

## Copy

- The headline carries the meaning.
- Supporting copy should usually be one short sentence.
- Give each section one job: explain, prove, deepen, or convert.
- Remove repetition aggressively.
- Do not leak prompt wording into the UI.

## Motion

- Use motion to create presence and hierarchy, not noise.
- A small number of intentional motions is better than many decorative ones.
- Fixed or floating elements must not overlap key content across breakpoints.

## Exceptions

- If a card, sticker, or metric row is essential to the brief, document why in the plan.
- If the hero needs more than one CTA or a more complex proof surface, explain the conversion logic explicitly.
