# Ship by Sunday List Building Plan

Last updated: 2026-03-11

## Goal

Define the smallest useful list-building system for `Ship by Sunday` so outreach can start cleanly later without turning this launch into a giant scraping project now.

This is a planning document, not a signal to scale outbound immediately.

## Operating rule

Do not build giant lists right now.

The correct order is:

1. define the right segments
2. define qualification rules
3. define the schema and personalization fields
4. collect a small sample set to validate fit and messaging
5. scale only after buyer flow, onboarding, and offer delivery are stable

## Core buyer

The highest-fit buyer is:

- builder who can already prototype or get things working
- has ideas or half-started projects
- wants leverage outside the day job
- keeps not finishing
- is interested in AI-assisted building, not pure theory
- would resonate with a software-guided sprint rather than a generic course

## Segment definitions

### Segment 1: Builders with side-project intent

Why they fit:

- they already have technical basics
- they want to prove they can ship something real
- they are likely to feel the pain of not finishing sharply
- they are more likely to buy structure than broad inspiration

Qualification criteria:

- title or public profile suggests early-career engineer, technical builder, or strong side-project operator
- public signals of side-project interest, AI-tool interest, hackathon behavior, or dev-tool curiosity
- evidence of unfinished or in-progress public work is a positive fit signal

Recommended angle:

- `You do not need more tutorials. You need a cleaner system for finishing a real v1.`

### Segment 2: Product-minded technical builders with unfinished ideas

Why they fit:

- they have enough skill to build useful software
- they often have stronger ambition but still lack a consistent shipping loop
- they may care about credibility, leverage, and proving range beyond their job

Qualification criteria:

- title suggests software engineer, product engineer, full-stack engineer, or platform engineer
- public interest in startups, SaaS, indie hacking, AI coding, or product building
- signs of side-project experimentation or builder identity

Recommended angle:

- `You can already build. The bottleneck is not just skill. It is product judgment, design direction, and execution structure.`

### Segment 3: Warm-adjacent technical builders

Why they fit:

- they are close enough to the target psychology even if not pure software engineers
- they may include technical PMs, product designers who build, or AI-native operators with real shipping intent
- they can provide strong early signal on message resonance

Qualification criteria:

- clear evidence of building behavior, not just consuming content
- public discussion of shipping, prototyping, tools, product ideas, or AI-assisted workflows
- enough technical fluency to plausibly use the course without getting lost

Recommended angle:

- `If you can already build enough to start, this gives you a path to actually close the loop.`

### Segment 4: Warm contacts and audience-adjacent people

Why they fit:

- highest reply probability
- easiest source of early learning
- useful for testing hooks before colder outbound

Qualification criteria:

- already knows Parker, Man Meets AI, or adjacent community
- has engaged with content before
- has visible technical or builder intent

Recommended angle:

- `I made something specifically for engineers who keep getting stuck before launch. I thought of you because this seems close to your world.`

## Exclusions

Do not target these groups in the first pass:

- total beginners
- broad “founders” with no technical fluency
- generic marketers or creators
- enterprise buyers
- people whose public behavior is mostly content consumption with no building intent
- people whose projects clearly require giant multi-system builds

## Warm vs warm-adjacent vs cold

### Warm

Definition:

- already knows Parker directly or has engaged meaningfully with Man Meets AI content

Examples:

- former collaborators
- community members
- prior customers
- newsletter readers who have replied
- people who have DM’d, emailed, or engaged repeatedly

Use case:

- test message resonance fast
- get first replies and objections
- learn which promise language lands

### Warm-adjacent

Definition:

- does not know Parker personally, but there is some contextual bridge

Examples:

- followers of adjacent builders
- mutual communities
- people who engage heavily with similar topics
- second-degree network connections

Use case:

- test offer fit outside the inner circle
- keep personalization relatively high

### Cold

Definition:

- no relationship and no contextual bridge beyond fit

Examples:

- engineers discovered from GitHub, X, LinkedIn, indie hacking communities, etc.

Use case:

- later-phase validation and controlled outbound only after launch flow is stable

## Qualification logic

A target is worth keeping if at least `3` of these are true:

- they are a technical builder or engineering-adjacent builder
- they publicly show side-project or builder intent
- they engage with AI coding, product building, or dev-tool content
- they show evidence of unfinished or experimental projects
- they would plausibly value a structured weekend sprint
- their public language suggests frustration with shipping or scope

A target should be deprioritized if any of these are true:

- they are obviously too senior and unlikely to buy a $499 product like this
- they are total beginners
- they mostly consume content but do not build
- their work suggests giant-team, enterprise-style projects only
- there is no realistic path from message to purchase

## List schema

Build lists with fields that support prioritization and personalization, not just contactability.

### Required columns

- `full_name`
- `first_name`
- `last_name`
- `email`
- `secondary_email` if available
- `company`
- `role_title`
- `segment`
- `warmth_category`
- `location`
- `linkedin_url`
- `github_url`
- `x_url`
- `personal_site`
- `evidence_of_fit`
- `builder_signal`
- `ai_signal`
- `side_project_signal`
- `likely_pain`
- `recommended_angle`
- `personalization_hook`
- `source`
- `source_quality`
- `confidence_score`
- `notes`

### Helpful optional columns

- `newsletter_engagement`
- `community_overlap`
- `mutual_connection`
- `project_link`
- `recent_post_title`
- `recent_repo_name`
- `reply_status`
- `owner`

## Source quality scale

Use a simple source-quality label:

- `high`: direct public profile, personal site, GitHub, LinkedIn, or warm relationship
- `medium`: community profile, public comments, indirect signal
- `low`: scraped data with weak context

Do not rely on low-quality sources for early outreach.

## Personalization angles by segment

### Junior engineers with side-project intent

Angle:

- finishing system
- not another tutorial
- clearer path from idea to shipped v1

Hooks:

- public side project
- AI tooling post
- “building in public” behavior

### Mid-level engineers with unfinished product ideas

Angle:

- sequence and scope discipline
- shipping outside the day job
- real leverage from finishing

Hooks:

- GitHub repos that look half-finished
- startup / SaaS curiosity
- comments about wanting to build faster

### Warm-adjacent technical builders

Angle:

- practical AI workflow
- clean weekend rep
- artifact-driven progress

Hooks:

- adjacent community participation
- shared tools or builder interests
- prior comments about not finishing

### Warm contacts

Angle:

- direct relevance
- builder pain
- “thought of you because this matches your world”

Hooks:

- specific memory of their project or content
- prior conversation
- clear bridge to the offer

## Example outreach hooks

These are not full emails. They are personalization starts.

- `Saw you’ve been shipping small AI experiments and thought this might be relevant.`
- `You seem exactly like the kind of engineer this was built for: technical enough to build, but still trying to close the loop faster.`
- `Noticed you’re working on side projects alongside your job. This is built for that exact problem.`
- `You mentioned using AI tools to move faster. This offer is less about tools and more about actually getting something live.`
- `You look like someone who does not need more information. You need a tighter shipping system.`

## Sample-target approach

Do this before any scale effort:

### Stage 1: warm sample

- collect `15-25` warm targets
- test the language and relevance manually
- log which hooks get replies

### Stage 2: warm-adjacent sample

- collect `25-40` warm-adjacent targets
- test the same core angles with lighter context
- compare fit and response quality

### Stage 3: cold sample

- collect `25-40` cold targets only after the buyer journey is stable
- keep segments tight
- do not mix all audiences in one campaign

## Priority ranking logic

Rank targets highest when:

- fit is obvious
- personalization is easy
- buying intent feels plausible
- technical level matches the product
- they have visible builder behavior

Rank targets lower when:

- the fit is generic
- the only signal is “works in tech”
- no specific pain or project angle is visible
- personalization would be fake or weak

## Recommendation

The list-building lane should produce:

- target segment definitions
- list schema
- a small warm sample
- a small warm-adjacent sample
- sample hooks

It should not produce:

- giant scraped lists
- mass enrichment work
- high-volume cold campaigns
- scale outbound execution before the launch flow is stable
