# Lesson 3.1 UX Critique: Lock Build Path and Scope

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to move a student from planning into live implementation without reopening every earlier decision. Lesson `3.1` is the first Build checkpoint, so it has to turn prior CEO and Design artifacts into one controlled starting brief instead of another abstract planning exercise.

### Surface goal

Lesson `3.1` should help the student choose one implementation path, define one first slice, define one visible done state, and leave with a prompt they can use immediately in `3.2` and beyond.

### Business model

This is a paid guided system, not a loose worksheet library. The first Build lesson has to feel like real operational guidance. If it feels generic, repetitive, or easy to misunderstand, students will lose confidence right when the course asks them to trust the system most.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `2.7` to `3.1` continuation rate
- `3.1` completion rate
- time from `3.1` load to prompt generation
- percentage of `3.2` starts where the `agent_build_brief` artifact is usable without revision
- support signals around “I still do not know what to build first”
- reopen rate on earlier Design lessons after entering Build

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: `3.1` is meant to reduce ambiguity, not teach setup mechanics yet.
- Assumption: the main deliverable is the generated build brief, not the three form fields by themselves.
- Assumption: most students entering this lesson already have the required Design artifacts, but still need help translating them into an implementation move.
- Confidence: high on the interaction and hierarchy issues because they are visible in the lesson contract and shared shell code; medium on behavioral severity because this pass did not include authenticated browser QA with real student data.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The lesson quietly seeds answer text before the student makes a decision.

User enters a lesson that is supposed to force three explicit Build decisions, but Steps 2 and 3 are prefilled with example copy on first load. That makes the workbench look more complete than the student’s thinking actually is, because `useBuildKickoffFlow` auto-writes default examples into `first_slice` and `definition_of_done`.

Impact:

- The progress state can feel artificially advanced before the student has done real work.
- Students may keep the sample text, lightly edit it, or mentally treat it as recommended truth.
- The product weakens the promise that Build starts with deliberate scope control.

### 2. The primary output is hidden behind a “generate” reveal instead of treated like the main artifact.

User’s true goal is to leave with a strong build brief, but the page frames the prompt as a secondary action at the bottom of the workbench. The prompt area is collapsed into an empty box until the student clicks `Generate build prompt`, which makes the artifact feel optional rather than central.

Impact:

- The lesson reads like a form first and a handoff lesson second.
- Students may complete the page without carefully reading the generated brief they are supposed to carry forward.
- The value of the system feels lower because the synthesis output is visually underweighted.

### 3. The path decision is too binary and under-explained for a high-risk transition.

User has to choose between `Use template` and `Custom path`, but the decision support is thin and mostly generic. The cards explain who each path is “best when” for, yet they do not surface the actual downstream consequences for repo setup, speed, or rework.

Impact:

- Students can choose a path without understanding the cost of being wrong.
- The lesson asks for commitment without enough confidence-building guidance.
- Later Build friction may feel like course confusion rather than a consequence of the chosen path.

### 4. Prior context is present, but buried in a collapsed `details` pattern.

User is asked to define a first slice from earlier artifacts, but the supporting context sits behind `Review your context` and depends on the student deciding to open it. For the first Build lesson, that makes the most valuable grounding information feel secondary.

Impact:

- Students may draft the slice from memory instead of from the actual product and design handoff.
- The lesson increases the chance of Build drift right at the handoff boundary.
- The course misses an opportunity to model how to actively use upstream artifacts while building.

### 5. The page reinforces step completion better than decision quality.

User can see which of the three steps are “Done,” but not whether the choices are actually good enough. The progress card and step cards reward any non-empty answer, while the lesson lacks a visible quality bar for what makes a strong first slice or a usable done condition.

Impact:

- Students can satisfy the UI while still writing vague or overly broad inputs.
- Weak briefs can move into `3.2` and feel “approved” by the course.
- The lesson teaches form completion more strongly than engineering judgment.

### 6. The shell splits attention across objective rail, workbench, and coach at the exact moment focus should tighten.

User arrives at the Build starting line, but the split layout spreads attention between the task rail, the workbench, and the coach panel. For a lesson whose main job is to make one concrete move, the chrome adds more simultaneous surfaces than the task really needs.

Impact:

- The page can feel heavier than the lesson content actually is.
- Students may bounce between recap, examples, and tasks rather than progressing cleanly through the decision sequence.
- On smaller laptop widths, the lesson risks feeling operationally busy instead of clarifying.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Replace seeded field text with explicit scaffolding.

What to change:

- Stop auto-writing example values into `first_slice` and `definition_of_done`.
- Keep examples as helper text, inline chips, or “show example” affordances that the student can inspect without inheriting.
- Reserve the `Done` state for student-authored input only.

Why it solves the user problem:

- The lesson regains honest progress feedback.
- Students have to make the decision themselves rather than edit inherited copy.

How it supports business growth:

- Stronger artifact quality at `3.1` should improve Build continuity and reduce confusion downstream.
- The product feels more rigorous and less template-ish.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Blank fields can feel slightly more effortful.
- The examples need to stay visible enough that the lesson does not become intimidating.

How to validate:

- QA whether students still complete the lesson at a healthy rate without seeded copy.
- Review saved `agent_build_brief` artifacts for originality and specificity before and after the change.

### 2. Make the generated brief the center of the page, not the epilogue.

What to change:

- Reframe the bottom card as `Your Build Brief`.
- Show a live preview that updates as the three decisions become available instead of hiding the prompt behind a reveal button.
- Keep `Copy prompt` as the final action, but make reading the artifact feel like the core moment.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It makes the lesson’s real deliverable visible and inspectable.
- Students can immediately see how path, slice, and done condition combine into one useful handoff.

How it supports business growth:

- The system’s value becomes tangible at the first Build lesson.
- Better prompt comprehension should reduce weak starts in later Build lessons.

Trade-offs or risks:

- A live prompt preview can add more text density.
- The prompt template must stay readable and not overwhelm the student.

How to validate:

- Measure whether prompt copy actions increase.
- Review whether students reference the brief more consistently in `3.2` support logs or QA sessions.

### 3. Add sharper decision support for the path choice.

What to change:

- Add one short comparison row under the path cards:
  - setup speed
  - flexibility
  - risk of drift
  - best fit
- Add a line that says what the next lesson will look like for each path.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It turns a generic preference choice into an informed implementation decision.
- Students can choose with clearer expectations about what comes next.

How it supports business growth:

- Better path choices should reduce avoidable friction in early Build.
- The product feels more expert-led because it explains consequences, not just options.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Slightly more copy in Step 1.
- The comparison needs to stay concrete and not become a mini essay.

How to validate:

- Ask during QA whether the correct path felt obvious.
- Compare later Build blockers between template-path and custom-path students.

### 4. Promote upstream artifacts from optional context to active decision input.

What to change:

- Show the two pinned artifacts open by default in `3.1`, at least until the student has written their first slice.
- Pull the most relevant details into a compact “Use this to decide your first slice” summary above Step 2.
- Consider showing only the most relevant fields instead of a full collapsible context block.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It keeps the student grounded in the actual handoff while defining the first Build move.
- The lesson becomes a translation step, not a memory test.

How it supports business growth:

- Better continuity between Design and Build strengthens the product’s credibility.
- Students should reopen prior lessons less often to remember what they decided.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much visible context can crowd the page.
- The artifact summary has to be selective or it will feel repetitive.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA whether students reference the visible artifact summary while writing the slice.
- Track whether earlier lesson reopen rates fall after the change.

### 5. Add a quality checkpoint before the brief is considered ready.

What to change:

- Add one short review card before the prompt:
  - Is the slice one screen or one workflow only?
  - Could someone else tell when it is done from the sentence alone?
  - Does the slice preserve the product and design decisions already made?
- If needed, soft-warn on vague phrases like “full flow,” “dashboard,” or “core app.”

Why it solves the user problem:

- It teaches Build judgment at the exact point the student needs it.
- The UI stops equating non-empty text with good decisions.

How it supports business growth:

- Higher-quality briefs improve downstream outcomes without adding another lesson.
- The course feels more like expert coaching and less like a checklist wrapper.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much validation could feel naggy if it is overly strict.
- Heuristics should guide, not falsely block good custom answers.

How to validate:

- Review saved artifacts for narrower slices and clearer done states.
- Check whether students need fewer retries or rewrites in `3.2`.

### 6. Consider a focus-first default for this lesson.

What to change:

- Default `3.1` to `focus` mode on first visit, with the objective rail and coach available as opt-in context.
- Keep split mode available, but treat it as secondary for users who want more simultaneous surfaces.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It tightens attention around the one workbench interaction that matters.
- The lesson feels more decisive and less like a dashboard.

How it supports business growth:

- A cleaner first Build experience reduces cognitive load at a fragile transition moment.
- The course feels more deliberate in how it controls pace and focus.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Some students like seeing the objective rail at all times.
- The lesson still needs a clear path to supporting context without burying it.

How to validate:

- QA perceived clarity in focus-first versus split-first sessions.
- Compare time-to-completion and prompt-generation rate.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

- The highest-value change is removing auto-seeded answers. It most directly restores honest decision-making.
- The second priority is promoting the generated brief to first-class status. That is the artifact the next lesson actually depends on.
- The lesson should stay short. Improvements should increase decisiveness, not add more ceremony.
- I would not add more fields. The right move is better scaffolding, better context exposure, and better artifact emphasis.

## Priority Plan

### Priority 1

- Remove default seeded values from `first_slice` and `definition_of_done`.
- Turn examples into explicit helper content instead of saved field state.

### Priority 2

- Redesign the prompt area into a live `Build Brief` preview with a stronger artifact hierarchy.
- Add a brief quality checkpoint before the artifact is considered ready.

### Priority 3

- Improve Step 1 path comparison with clearer downstream consequences.
- Promote upstream artifact context higher in the flow.

### Priority 4

- Test a focus-first default layout for `3.1`.

## Validation Plan

- Run browser QA on lesson `3.1` with realistic pinned artifacts and inspect the full flow from `2.7` into `3.2`.
- Compare saved `agent_build_brief` artifacts before and after the change for specificity, scope tightness, and readability.
- Measure prompt generation and copy behavior to confirm the output is being used.
- Review support and QA notes for early Build confusion, especially around slice size and path choice.

## Narrative Summary

Lesson `3.1` has the right structural intent: narrow the Build starting point to one path, one slice, and one done condition. The current implementation gets the student through that sequence, but it still behaves more like a well-styled form than a sharp Build handoff. The biggest problems are that the lesson pre-populates critical answers, hides its real output until the end, and treats upstream context as optional reading instead of active decision input. Tightening those three areas would make this lesson feel like the real start of Build, not just the administrative prelude to it.
