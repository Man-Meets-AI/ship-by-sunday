# Lesson 2.4 UX Critique: Figma Basics for Speed

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to get a paying student from idea to shipped v1 over one guided weekend. The product works when each lesson removes one real source of friction, makes progress feel tangible, and compounds into the next step without forcing the student to re-interpret the method.

### Surface goal

Lesson `2.4` should remove tool anxiety right before students start building rough screens in `2.5`. This surface needs to make Figma feel survivable, prove the learner opened the right file and understands the minimum controls, and turn the storyboard from `2.3` into a live starting point instead of a dead artifact.

### Business model

This is a paid, gated course product sold through Stripe, authenticated with Clerk, and persisted in Convex. The business depends on activation, weekend momentum, continuation between lessons, and a strong feeling that the course reduces shipping friction rather than adding one more layer of confusion.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `2.3` to `2.4` continuation rate
- `2.4` completion rate
- `2.4` to `2.5` continuation rate
- time from `2.4` load to first real action in `2.5`
- percentage of students who arrive in `2.5` with an actual file open and usable
- support/refund signals around “do I need to know Figma first?” or “what file am I supposed to open?”
- percentage of students who complete `2.4` without later attaching evidence that a Figma file exists

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: this lesson is intentionally a thin calibration step, not a full Figma tutorial.
- Assumption: the actual walkthrough video and transcript are still pending, so the current experience relies on placeholder teaching and resource links.
- Assumption: `2.5` depends on the artifact key `figma_file_ready`, so the product intends this lesson to create a real handoff artifact rather than a symbolic completion state.
- Confidence: high on the structural issues around completion logic, proof of work, and handoff clarity because they are explicit in the lesson contract, tests, and Convex completion path; medium on exact severity because this pass did not include live browser QA or analytics review.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The lesson can be completed without opening Figma at all.

User cannot trust that `2.4` actually means “file ready,” causing a fake handoff into `2.5`, because the lesson is explicitly coded as a watch-only lesson with no required in-product fields, no objective tasks, and no completion issues. The footer CTA is effectively enabled on load, and `completeLesson` accepts completion with empty `fieldValues`.

Impact:

- Students can unlock `2.5` by clicking `Complete lesson` without opening the file, practicing any controls, or proving anything about the Figma setup.
- The artifact label `Figma File Ready` over-promises relative to what the system validates.
- The course teaches symbolic progression at the exact moment it should be reducing real tool friction.

### 2. The page promises “Watch first,” but the most important teaching surface is a placeholder.

User cannot calibrate what “minimum viable Figma skill” actually looks like, causing hesitation or skip behavior, because the watch shell leads with the lesson video area while the current state is a generic placeholder rather than a real walkthrough. This lesson depends more on demonstration than explanation.

Impact:

- First-time Figma users do not get the fast confidence transfer this lesson is supposed to provide.
- The screen opens with a broken-feeling primary action.
- Students are more likely to skip the lesson or mark it complete without gaining the intended comfort.

### 3. The lesson does not translate the storyboard into a concrete Figma starting move.

User cannot tell how the saved storyboard should drive the file setup, causing a weak bridge from `2.3`, because the watch shell for `2.4` does not surface pinned artifacts and the checklist stays generic: open file, learn controls, confirm ready. The storyboard dependency exists in the data model, but not in the visible teaching.

Impact:

- `2.4` feels detached from the previous lesson even though `storyboard_image` is the only dependency.
- Students may open a blank file without knowing which frames or states they are meant to recreate first.
- The Design block loses the sense of compounding forward from sketch to storyboard to rough screens.

### 4. The required output is too abstract for a tool-setup lesson.

User cannot confidently judge whether they are done, causing shallow or anxious completion, because `Figma File Ready` is an internal artifact label rather than a student-legible proof state. There is no visible checklist like “correct file opened,” “storyboard beside it,” or “one frame duplicated and renamed.”

Impact:

- Learners do not know what “ready” means in observable terms.
- Confident users may breeze through without establishing a reusable setup.
- Nervous users may over-prepare because the finish line is vague.

### 5. The resource path is too thin for a lesson centered on one external tool.

User cannot see one obvious, durable entry path into the right workspace, causing avoidable friction, because the watch shell exposes only generic resources and the workbench variant that could hold richer external-workspace guidance is bypassed for watch lessons. The lesson says “move into the Figma file,” but the visible shell does not do much to operationalize that step.

Impact:

- Students may not know whether to use a template, create a file, duplicate an existing file, or open a specific board.
- The interface feels less intentional than surrounding lessons with more explicit artifact framing.
- The product spends an entire lesson on Figma without giving the tool-switch enough structure.

### 6. The transition into `2.5` is operationally weak.

User can clear `2.4` without a ready-to-build setup, causing `2.5` to absorb avoidable onboarding friction, because the completion banner only celebrates the generic artifact lock and next lesson unlock. It does not say what should now exist in Figma before rough screens begin.

Impact:

- `2.5` may start with “wait, which file?” instead of “start building the first rough screen.”
- The course loses some of its promise that each lesson narrows the next move.
- Students who were already tool-anxious enter the next lesson still cold.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Add one real proof-of-readiness gate before completion.

What to change:

- Add a compact required confirmation block for `2.4` with checks such as:
  - `I opened the Figma file I will use for rough screens`
  - `I can pan, zoom, duplicate, and rename a frame`
  - `I have my storyboard or sketches beside the file`
- Require the checks before enabling completion.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It turns the artifact from symbolic progress into observable readiness.
- It keeps the lesson lightweight while preventing empty completion.

How it supports business growth:

- Better `2.4` completion quality should improve `2.5` start confidence and reduce friction in the design sequence.
- It preserves trust that locked artifacts mean something real.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Adds a little friction to a lesson meant to feel fast.
- If the checks are too vague, they become another symbolic checkbox set.

How to validate:

- Compare `2.4 -> 2.5` continuation and first-action speed in `2.5`.
- QA whether users can explain what they physically did in Figma before completing the lesson.

### 2. Make the storyboard visibly drive the Figma setup.

What to change:

- Surface the saved `storyboard_image` in the watch shell or add a compact “Use this as your source” panel above resources.
- Add direct copy that says:
  - `Open your Figma file with the storyboard beside it.`
  - `You are not inventing new flow here. You are rebuilding the saved journey one frame at a time.`

Why it solves the user problem:

- It turns the previous artifact into an active input.
- It reduces the feeling that Figma starts from scratch.

How it supports business growth:

- Stronger continuity across lessons improves perceived product coherence.
- Better setup quality should lead to cleaner rough-screen work in `2.5`.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Extra context could clutter a lesson that should stay slim.
- The surfaced artifact needs to stay concise and clearly subordinate to the main action.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA whether users can describe how `2.3` feeds `2.4` after one glance.
- Review whether `2.5` artifacts more consistently follow the storyboard sequence.

### 3. Replace the placeholder-first feel with stronger fallback teaching until media exists.

What to change:

- If the video is still missing, replace the current empty watch state with a concrete fallback card that teaches:
  - the 4-5 controls that matter
  - the exact goal of this lesson
  - what “ready” looks like before moving on
- Ideally attach one short walkthrough clip or GIF as soon as possible.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It restores trust in the primary lesson surface.
- It gives anxious learners a workable substitute for a live demo.

How it supports business growth:

- Better calibration at this step protects continuation through the Design block.
- The course feels more complete and intentional, which matters in a paid product.

Trade-offs or risks:

- A text fallback cannot fully replace a real demo.
- A too-dense fallback card could make the lesson feel heavier than it should.

How to validate:

- Compare completion and continuation once media or richer fallback teaching is added.
- Ask QA users whether they feel they could open Figma confidently after this lesson alone.

### 4. Redefine the output in student language, not system language.

What to change:

- Replace or supplement `Figma File Ready` with a more observable output label such as:
  - `Working Figma file opened and ready for rough screens`
  - `Figma setup complete`
- In the completion copy, say what should now exist: the file is open, the student can move around it, and the storyboard is the reference.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It makes the finish line legible.
- It helps the student judge readiness by behavior rather than internal platform wording.

How it supports business growth:

- Clearer task framing reduces user hesitation and support questions.
- Better output language improves trust in the course’s step-by-step system.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Copy changes alone will not fix the false-completion problem without validation changes.
- Too much wording can make a fast lesson feel ceremonial.

How to validate:

- Ask QA users what they believe “done” means before and after the copy change.
- Review whether fewer students enter `2.5` still asking where to work.

### 5. Give the external tool jump a single obvious path.

What to change:

- Add one primary action card that makes the tool switch explicit:
  - `Open the Figma file`
  - `Keep storyboard visible`
  - `Practice these 4 controls`
- If there is a canonical Figma template or file, surface it as the dominant action rather than burying it in generic resources.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It removes ambiguity around where to go and what to do there.
- It gives the lesson a clearer operational spine.

How it supports business growth:

- Lower tool-switch friction should improve real completion quality.
- The product feels more polished and guided during a fragile point in the student journey.

Trade-offs or risks:

- If the primary file path is wrong or unstable, this card will magnify confusion.
- The lesson should not become a file-management screen.

How to validate:

- QA whether first-time users can reach the correct Figma workspace without exploring other panels.
- Track clicks on the primary tool-open action if instrumentation exists.

### 6. Make the `2.5` handoff operational instead of ceremonial.

What to change:

- Update the completion banner and footer to say what the learner should do next, for example:
  - `Next: rebuild the first 3-5 storyboard frames as rough screens in this same file.`
- If the proof gate is added, echo the readiness state in the celebration copy.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It converts completion into a direct next action.
- It keeps momentum through the tool transition.

How it supports business growth:

- Better cross-lesson continuity improves activation and perceived instructional quality.
- Students are less likely to stall between setup and actual screen-making.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much future-facing copy can distract from finishing the current lesson.
- The message must stay blunt and short.

How to validate:

- Compare `2.4 -> 2.5` progression rate and time-to-first rough-screen evidence.
- Ask QA users what they expect to do in `2.5` before opening it.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

- Keep this lesson thin. The right move is not building in-app Figma training, but adding proof of readiness, better fallback teaching, and a stronger bridge from storyboard to file.
- Validate behavior, not just page view. Because `2.4` exists to remove tool friction, completion should mean “the student can now work,” not “the student saw the lesson.”
- Reuse prior artifacts as active context. The storyboard dependency should shape the teaching directly instead of remaining only in the data model.
- Favor one obvious external-tool path. This lesson is about removing hesitation, so tool-choice ambiguity and placeholder-first hierarchy are both costly.

## Priority Plan

### Must

- Add a real completion gate so `2.4` cannot be cleared without explicit proof of readiness.
- Surface the storyboard as an active input to the Figma setup.
- Replace the placeholder-first experience with stronger fallback teaching until media is attached.
- Make the `2.5` handoff concrete in completion copy.

### Should

- Rename or supplement the artifact/output language so “ready” is legible to students.
- Add one primary external-tool action card if a canonical Figma file or template exists.

### Could

- Instrument “open Figma file” and “complete readiness gate” events once analytics are in place.
- Add a micro-demo GIF for the few controls that matter most if full video is delayed.

## Validation Plan

- Browser-QA the live `2.3 -> 2.4 -> 2.5` flow with a real entitled account.
- Confirm the `Complete lesson` CTA is disabled until the new readiness proof is satisfied.
- Confirm users can explain how the storyboard should be used once Figma opens.
- Confirm users know exactly which file or template to open without detouring into other panels.
- Once analytics exist, compare `2.4` completion quality, `2.4 -> 2.5` continuation, and time-to-first-action in `2.5` before and after the UX pass.

## Narrative Summary

Lesson `2.4` has the right intent but currently behaves like a symbolic unlock, not a real readiness step. The code path makes that explicit: the lesson is a watch-only shell with no required tasks or fields, so students can clear a supposedly tool-prep artifact without opening Figma once. That breaks trust in the artifact system and weakens the setup for `2.5`.

The fix should stay small. `2.4` does not need to become Figma school. It needs one observable readiness gate, stronger fallback teaching while media is missing, a visible bridge from the saved storyboard into the file, and completion copy that turns the next lesson into an obvious continuation. If the student leaves `2.4` with the right file open, the storyboard beside it, and enough confidence to start rough screens immediately, the lesson will finally match its purpose.
