# Lesson 2.3 UX Critique: Storyboard the Journey

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to move a paying student from loose idea to a shipped v1 in one guided weekend. The product works when each lesson narrows uncertainty, creates visible progress, and makes the next lesson easier to start without re-explaining the method.

### Surface goal

Lesson `2.3` should turn the rough sketches from `2.2` into one ordered, buildable user journey. This screen needs to help the student choose the right path, sequence it into `5-7` frames, and return with a storyboard artifact that makes the `2.4` Figma step feel obvious instead of abstract.

### Business model

This is a paid, in-app course product sold through Stripe, gated through Clerk, and persisted in Convex. The business depends on activation, continuation through the weekend sprint, low confusion between lessons, and a strong sense that the course improves product judgment rather than just collecting files.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `2.2` to `2.3` continuation rate
- `2.3` completion rate
- `2.3` to `2.4` continuation rate
- time from `2.3` load to first storyboard upload
- time from upload to lesson completion
- percentage of `2.4` students who can start Figma without reopening `2.2`
- support/refund signals around “what counts as a storyboard?” or “which template should I use?”

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: students arrive with a saved `sketch_set` from `2.2`, and that artifact preview is visible in the workbench.
- Assumption: the lesson is intentionally manual-first and should not become an in-app whiteboard or storyboard editor.
- Assumption: video and transcript are still pending for this lesson, so the critique must account for a coach surface that cannot currently demonstrate the rep.
- Confidence: high on structural issues around hierarchy, completion logic, and handoff clarity because they are explicit in the lesson contract and component code; medium on exact severity because this pass did not include browser QA or live analytics.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The lesson asks for sequencing work, but the product only verifies file presence.

User cannot tell whether the storyboard is actually good enough, causing weak downstream inputs, because the coded completion bar is one required `storyboard_upload` while the instructional promise is “turn rough screens into one ordered user journey.” The system checks storage, not sequencing quality.

Impact:

- Students can complete `2.3` with a blurry or incomplete artifact and still unlock `2.4`.
- The product teaches “upload anything” more strongly than “clarify the flow.”
- Figma inherits ambiguity that this lesson is supposed to remove.

### 2. The saved `2.2` sketch context is visible, but it is not converted into an explicit choice.

User cannot confidently decide which path to storyboard, causing hesitation or over-inclusion, because the pinned `Sketch Set` preview is passive context rather than an active prompt to pick one path, one outcome moment, and one version of the journey.

Impact:

- Students may try to carry every sketch variation into the storyboard.
- The lesson risks duplicating `2.2` instead of sharpening it.
- The Design block loses momentum because the transition from “rough screens” to “single sequence” is implied instead of taught.

### 3. The tool path is split across two places, which makes the lesson feel less certain than it should.

User cannot immediately tell which medium to use, causing avoidable decision friction, because the primary workbench surfaces only the printable storyboard worksheet while the FigJam option lives separately in the coach resources panel. The copy says PDF, FigJam, or plain paper all count, but the interface favors one option and hides another.

Impact:

- Students who prefer digital work may miss the FigJam path.
- The product looks less deliberate because the allowed paths are not grouped in one obvious chooser.
- First-run confidence drops at the exact moment the lesson should feel lightweight.

### 4. The page is dominated by upload mechanics rather than by a storyboarding framework.

User can act, but not think clearly through the task, causing superficial work, because the workbench goes from header to template to status rail to upload field without a compact scaffold for “start state, key transition, value moment, finish state.” The artifact submission step is more concrete than the story-structuring step.

Impact:

- Students may draw boxes without understanding what each frame should prove.
- The lesson feels administrative even though it is supposed to improve narrative and flow clarity.
- The product under-delivers on the promise of product judgment coaching.

### 5. The handoff into `2.4` is too abrupt.

User can complete `2.3` without understanding how this artifact should drive the Figma step, causing a weak transition, because the current win condition says the storyboard is “ready for the Figma step,” but the screen does not explain what in the storyboard should be carried over once the learner lands in `2.4`.

Impact:

- `2.4` can feel like a reset instead of the next layer of the same decision.
- Students may open Figma and still wonder which frames or states matter.
- The product loses some of its “one guided weekend sprint” coherence.

### 6. Missing media hurts this lesson more than a pure reference lesson.

User cannot calibrate pace or expected roughness, causing more anxiety, because `2.3` is a “show me the rep” lesson and the coach video/transcript are both pending. Without a live walkthrough or even a compact example, the interface asks the student to infer what “5-7 frames in order” really looks like.

Impact:

- Students who are unsure how rough is acceptable may stall or overwork the artifact.
- The lesson opens with lower trust than it should.
- The course feels less finished right before the tool-heavy Figma sequence begins.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Add a lightweight storyboard quality gate before completion.

What to change:

- Add a short required confirmation block near the upload area with checks such as:
  - `Shows one core path`
  - `Frames are in order`
  - `The value moment is visible`
  - `Someone else could follow this sequence`
- Keep it compact and manual-first; this does not need scoring or AI review.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It defines “done” in the language of sequence and clarity instead of storage.
- It helps students self-correct before locking a weak artifact.

How it supports business growth:

- Better storyboard quality should improve `2.4` start confidence and continuation.
- It reinforces the product as a judgment-building system, not a document vault.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Adds a little friction to a short lesson.
- If the gate is too long, it will feel like bureaucracy.

How to validate:

- Compare `2.3 -> 2.4` continuation and time-to-first-action in `2.4`.
- Review a sample of saved storyboard uploads before and after the change.

### 2. Turn the pinned `Sketch Set` into an explicit narrowing prompt.

What to change:

- Add a compact card above the upload field that says, in effect:
  - `Pick the one path these frames should tell`
  - `Carry forward the strongest flow, not every variant`
  - `Make the outcome moment unmissable`
- Optionally surface one sentence summary generated from saved `1.2`/`1.3` context: who this is for and what they need to achieve.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It converts passive context into an actual decision.
- It makes the storyboard feel like a narrowing move, not another generic artifact step.

How it supports business growth:

- Better continuity across lessons increases trust in the method.
- Cleaner storyboards reduce downstream confusion and support burden.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much context could crowd a lesson that should stay fast.
- The prompt must stay narrow or it will become another reading task.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA whether first-time users can explain what changed from `2.2` to `2.3`.
- Review whether uploaded boards show one clearer path instead of many competing branches.

### 3. Combine the allowed storyboard tools into one obvious choice surface.

What to change:

- Replace the single template link with a compact “Choose your format” card that lists:
  - printable worksheet
  - FigJam template
  - plain paper / tablet / Excalidraw
- Keep the worksheet and FigJam links both present in the main workbench instead of splitting them between the template card and coach resources.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It removes tool-path ambiguity up front.
- It lets students choose a medium fast and move off-screen.

How it supports business growth:

- Lower setup friction should improve lesson start rate and continuation.
- The product feels more intentional and complete.

Trade-offs or risks:

- More options can create hesitation if the card becomes too heavy.
- The card should emphasize that all paths are equivalent, not that the student must evaluate software choices.

How to validate:

- Track clicks on worksheet vs FigJam once both are surfaced together.
- Ask QA users whether they know all acceptable storyboard paths without opening coach resources.

### 4. Add a tiny story-structure scaffold before the upload.

What to change:

- Add a simple frame-planning prompt inline, for example:
  - `Frame 1: starting state`
  - `Frames 2-4: key actions or state changes`
  - `Frame 5+: value moment and finish`
- Keep it as teaching scaffolding, not a new form to fill out.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It gives students a mental model for sequencing before they leave the app.
- It reduces blank-page friction without turning the lesson into software.

How it supports business growth:

- Faster starts and better artifacts protect activation through the Design block.
- The product feels more like coaching and less like homework submission.

Trade-offs or risks:

- If over-explained, it will slow down the rep.
- The scaffold must support different products without forcing one exact story shape.

How to validate:

- Test with students who say they are “not designers” and see if they start faster.
- Compare storyboard clarity in artifact reviews after the scaffold ships.

### 5. Make the `2.4` handoff explicit at completion time.

What to change:

- After upload, show a summary line such as:
  - `Next, rebuild these frames in Figma. Use the storyboard to preserve order, state changes, and the value moment.`
- If feasible, echo the artifact role in the footer completion area so the CTA explains what the storyboard is for, not just that the next lesson is Figma.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It translates the artifact into the next action.
- It reduces the feeling that each lesson starts from scratch.

How it supports business growth:

- Better handoffs improve continuation and perceived instructional quality.
- The course feels more systematized, which supports retention and referrals.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much forward-looking copy can steal focus from the current lesson.
- The handoff line must stay concrete and short.

How to validate:

- Measure `2.3 -> 2.4` continuation and first meaningful action in `2.4`.
- Ask QA users what they expect to do in Figma before opening the next lesson.

### 6. Treat missing video or example media as a real lesson-quality gap.

What to change:

- Prioritize attaching either:
  - the real walkthrough video, or
  - one compact example image of a “good enough” storyboard
- If media is still pending, add stronger inline fallback teaching in the main workbench instead of leaving calibration mostly to the hidden coach surface.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It gives anxious learners a clearer quality bar.
- It makes the intended pace and roughness easier to understand.

How it supports business growth:

- Better calibration reduces abandonment in the Design block.
- The lesson feels more complete and trustable.

Trade-offs or risks:

- A too-polished example could make learners think their version needs polish.
- The fallback must model rough clarity, not beauty.

How to validate:

- Track whether upload times shorten after example media appears.
- Watch whether support questions about “what counts?” decline.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

- Keep the lesson manual-first. The right fix is better framing, quality gating, and handoff clarity, not an in-app storyboard editor.
- Favor one narrowing decision over more fields. `2.3` should help students choose and sequence one path, not create another form-heavy checkpoint.
- Put tool choices in the main workbench. Because medium choice is part of getting started, it should not depend on opening coach resources.
- Raise the quality bar without adding heavy friction. A short checklist and a tiny scaffold are the right trade-off; anything more complex would slow the weekend sprint down.

## Priority Plan

### Must

- Add a lightweight storyboard quality gate tied to sequence clarity, not just upload presence.
- Make the `2.2` to `2.3` narrowing move explicit by telling the student to pick one path from the saved sketch set.
- Surface all valid storyboard formats in one primary chooser in the workbench.
- Make the `2.4` Figma handoff explicit in completion and next-step copy.

### Should

- Add a compact frame-planning scaffold that explains how to structure `5-7` frames.
- Add stronger inline fallback teaching or one rough example while lesson media is still pending.

### Could

- Personalize the lesson with a one-line “our user / promised outcome” reminder pulled from earlier artifacts.
- Add artifact-review instrumentation later to assess storyboard quality trends by lesson completion cohort.

## Validation Plan

- Browser-QA the live `2.2 -> 2.3 -> 2.4` flow on desktop and mobile with a real entitled account.
- Confirm that students can identify all valid storyboard formats without opening coach resources.
- Confirm that the saved `Sketch Set` clearly anchors the storyboard decision instead of feeling like passive decoration.
- Check whether completion now requires both a stored upload and a sequence-quality confirmation.
- Once analytics exist, compare `2.3` completion, `2.3 -> 2.4` continuation, and time-to-first-action in `2.4` before and after the UX pass.

## Narrative Summary

Lesson `2.3` is directionally right: it correctly keeps storyboarding off-screen, keeps the artifact simple, and preserves a hard dependency before Figma. The weakness is that the experience currently behaves like a file-return checkpoint when it should behave like a narrative decision point. Students are asked to transform rough sketches into one clear journey, but the product mainly verifies that they uploaded something.

The best next iteration is not more software. It is a clearer narrowing move, one obvious format chooser, a tiny sequence scaffold, and a real quality bar that matches the lesson’s purpose. If `2.3` makes “pick one path, show the value moment, then rebuild this in Figma” feel unmistakable, the whole Design block will read as one compounding method instead of three adjacent upload steps.
