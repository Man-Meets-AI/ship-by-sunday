# Lesson 2.7 UX Critique: Design Wrap-Up

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to move a paying student from idea and early design into a real shipped v1 over one guided weekend. Each lesson should reduce uncertainty, preserve momentum, and leave behind an artifact the next block can use without reinterpretation.

### Surface goal

Lesson `2.7` should convert the Design block into a trustworthy Build handoff. It needs to reassure the student that their references, sketches, storyboard, rough screens, and `visualguide.md` now add up to one usable spec, then make the jump into `3.1` feel obvious and earned.

### Business model

This is a paid, guided course product. The lesson should feel like expert compression of prior work, not a ceremonial gate. If the wrap-up feels flimsy, students will question whether the system is actually leading them into Build or just marking progress complete.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `2.6` to `2.7` continuation rate
- `2.7` completion rate
- `2.7` to `3.1` continuation rate
- time from `2.7` page load to lesson completion
- percentage of Build starts with all required Design artifacts present and intelligible
- support/refund signals around “I still did not know what to build next”
- percentage of `3.1` entries where students reopen earlier Design lessons before starting

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: `2.7` is intentionally a stage-clear lesson, not a heavy editing step.
- Assumption: the main job is to help students verify handoff quality, not generate net-new design work.
- Assumption: the current summary data comes only from saved upstream artifacts and does not inspect deeper artifact quality.
- Confidence: high on the structural issues because they are visible in the lesson contract, stage-unlock summary builder, and renderer; medium on behavioral severity because this pass did not include authenticated browser QA with real student data.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The hero promises success before the student has actually earned it.

User lands on a page whose main headline already says `Build unlocked`, causing premature closure, because the hero copy is written in post-completion language even though the actual completion action still sits in the footer and the lesson can still be incomplete.

Impact:

- The page reads more like a celebration screen than a decision screen.
- Students may skim instead of verifying whether the handoff is actually usable.
- The product weakens its own credibility by declaring the next block unlocked before the system has actually completed the transition.

### 2. The summary compresses artifact presence well, but handoff quality poorly.

User can see that references, sketches, storyboard, and guide exist, but cannot quickly judge whether they are strong enough to build from, because the stage-unlock summary mostly reports counts and high-level statements rather than surfacing the critical details Build will depend on.

Impact:

- The student gets artifact confirmation, but not true implementation confidence.
- `2.7` can feel like “yes, files exist” instead of “yes, Build can start cleanly.”
- Weak upstream work can pass through this lesson without meaningful inspection.

### 3. Rough screens are required for completion, but underrepresented in the page itself.

User is told rough screens matter, yet the page does not visibly prove what those screens are or why they are enough, causing a mismatch between the checklist and the lived summary, because the summary includes a generic “Screen plan” section with no preview, no screen inventory, and no explicit tie back to the saved rough-screen artifact.

Impact:

- One of the most implementation-relevant Design outputs feels abstracted away.
- Students may not trust that Build really has a usable screen plan.
- The lesson misses a chance to reinforce the exact screens the student should carry into `3.1`.

### 4. The page lacks a concrete bridge into the first Build lesson.

User knows Build is next, but not exactly how to start it, causing a motivational and operational gap, because `2.7` includes a generic “Next: BUILD 3.1” label and a “Skills you flexed” card but no Build-facing preflight, no “what to do in the first 10 minutes,” and no explicit reminder of what should stay fixed as implementation begins.

Impact:

- The transition from Design into Build feels ceremonial rather than guided.
- Students can enter `3.1` still wondering what must stay stable versus what can change.
- A crucial cross-block handoff becomes softer than earlier intra-block handoffs.

### 5. The review-and-lock experience is thinner than the lesson framing implies.

User is told to “lock the spec,” but the page provides no dedicated explicit review step for the Design preset, causing a gap between narrative and interaction, because the stage-unlock workbench only shows the review card when a `brief_review_checks` field exists and `2.7` does not define one.

Impact:

- The main behavior becomes “read the recap, then hit Unlock Build.”
- There is no visible self-check that asks whether the design spec is actually implementation-ready.
- The product misses a chance to teach what a strong build handoff looks like.

### 6. The current layout spends prime space on celebration and recap, not the one decision that matters.

User’s main job is to decide “is Build genuinely set up to begin,” but the page allocates most of its above-the-fold area to celebratory hero copy and passive summary cards, causing weak action hierarchy, because the primary completion action lives in the footer while the body has no dominant decision module.

Impact:

- The page feels more like a milestone page than a control point.
- Students on smaller screens may read and scroll without a strong sense of what to do now.
- A short lesson can still feel oddly diffuse because summary density replaces decision clarity.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Change the hero from premature celebration to preflight confirmation.

What to change:

- Replace `Build unlocked` with a title like `Confirm the Build Handoff`.
- Keep the post-completion celebration language for the completion banner only.
- Reframe the hero body around one question: “Would Build know what to make first without rethinking the product?”

Why it solves the user problem:

- It matches the lesson’s actual state when the student first arrives.
- It puts the student into review mode instead of passive acceptance.

How it supports business growth:

- Stronger perceived rigor at the block boundary increases trust in the product.
- Students should enter Build with less drift and less need to backtrack.

Trade-offs or risks:

- The page may feel slightly less immediately exciting.
- The celebratory moment has to land later, so the completion banner needs to feel satisfying.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA whether users pause to inspect the summary before completing.
- Compare `2.7 -> 3.1` continuation and back-navigation rates after the change.

### 2. Turn the summary from proof-of-existence into proof-of-readiness.

What to change:

- Expand each section to show the most implementation-relevant detail:
  - references: the key patterns to borrow
  - rough screens: the actual screen list or short labels
  - storyboard: the flow order in words, not just an image
  - visual guide: 3-4 concrete rules excerpted from `visualguide.md`
- Keep counts secondary and decision-ready content primary.

Why it solves the user problem:

- Students can judge Build readiness from the page itself.
- The lesson becomes a real synthesis step instead of a status recap.

How it supports business growth:

- Better handoffs should reduce confusion in `3.1`.
- The product feels more opinionated and valuable when it surfaces the right details automatically.

Trade-offs or risks:

- More content can make the page longer if the excerpts are not tightly edited.
- Pulling too much content forward can make the page feel repetitive with prior lessons.

How to validate:

- QA whether a student could explain their first build slice using only the `2.7` page.
- Sample saved artifacts and inspect whether the `2.7` summary now exposes enough to guide implementation.

### 3. Give rough screens a first-class presence in the handoff.

What to change:

- Add a dedicated rough-screens section with thumbnail preview or named screen list.
- Make the stats row include a rough-screens signal, since it is required for completion.
- Tie the section copy directly to Build: “These are the screens you implement first.”

Why it solves the user problem:

- It restores one of the core handoff artifacts to visible importance.
- It makes the screen plan legible without reopening `2.5`.

How it supports business growth:

- Better Build starts should improve perceived course continuity.
- Students should spend less time re-orienting themselves between blocks.

Trade-offs or risks:

- If `rough_screens` data is sparse, previews may look weak unless the upstream artifact is also tightened.
- Thumbnail-heavy layout could crowd the page on smaller screens.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA the live `2.7` page with real rough-screen data on desktop and mobile.
- Check whether students still reopen `2.5` before starting `3.1`.

### 4. Replace the “Skills you flexed” ending with a Build start card.

What to change:

- Remove or demote `Skills you flexed`.
- Add a final card called `Start Build with this fixed` or `What stays stable in 3.1`.
- Include 3 bullets:
  - what Build should not re-decide
  - what the first slice should probably implement
  - when it is valid to go back and revise Design

Why it solves the user problem:

- It turns the bottom of the page into a forward bridge instead of a retrospective flourish.
- It tells the student exactly how to carry momentum into the next lesson.

How it supports business growth:

- Students should feel led across the block boundary, not just congratulated.
- Better transition clarity should reduce abandonment between Design and Build.

Trade-offs or risks:

- The page becomes more prescriptive about how Build starts.
- Some students may want more freedom, so the guidance should preserve a small escape hatch for legitimate redesigns.

How to validate:

- Observe whether students begin `3.1` faster and with less hesitation.
- Review support questions for fewer “what do I do now?” signals after `2.7`.

### 5. Add an explicit Design handoff review checklist.

What to change:

- Define `brief_review_checks` for the Design preset and render a review card tailored to Build readiness.
- Example checks:
  - the first build slice is visible in the screens and storyboard
  - the visual guide contains concrete repeatable UI rules
  - nothing in the spec quietly widens the v1 again
- Update the review-card copy so it refers to Build handoff quality, not reference gathering.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It makes “lock the spec” a real interaction instead of a vague instruction.
- It teaches students how to self-audit their handoff before building.

How it supports business growth:

- Higher artifact quality should reduce failure in the Build block.
- The product feels more like guided judgment, less like serialized content.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too many checks can make a short wrap-up lesson feel bureaucratic.
- Reusing one generic review component may still produce copy drift unless preset-specific text is added.

How to validate:

- Measure whether explicit review checks reduce weak `3.1` starts.
- Watch whether completion drop-off changes materially after adding the checklist.

### 6. Bring the primary action up into the body of the page.

What to change:

- Add a dominant body-level `Ready to unlock Build` panel near the end of the summary.
- Show completion status and the final CTA there, not only in the footer.
- Keep the footer CTA for consistency, but make the page’s decision point visible before the last scroll stop.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It aligns the page’s interaction hierarchy with the lesson’s actual job.
- It helps short wrap-up lessons feel deliberate rather than passive.

How it supports business growth:

- Students should complete the block with less friction on mobile and smaller laptops.
- The page will feel more intentionally designed around momentum.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Duplicate CTAs can feel redundant if the placement is not visually coordinated.
- The body CTA should mirror footer state exactly to avoid confusion.

How to validate:

- Browser-QA completion behavior on desktop and mobile.
- Measure whether time-to-complete drops without reducing artifact quality.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

- Decision: preserve `2.7` as a short wrap-up lesson rather than turning it into another heavy workbench. The fix should strengthen verification and transition clarity, not add more production work.
- Decision: prioritize Build-readiness signals over retrospective celebration. Celebration should happen after completion, not before.
- Decision: use the page to surface the most useful implementation details automatically from saved artifacts wherever possible, instead of asking the student to restate prior work.
- Trade-off: adding richer previews and checks increases density, so the page will need stronger hierarchy and tighter copy to avoid becoming another wall of recap.

## Priority Plan

### Priority 1

- Reframe the hero as preflight confirmation, not already-unlocked celebration.
- Add a Build-specific review checklist and body-level action panel.
- Replace the bottom “Skills you flexed” card with a real bridge into `3.1`.

### Priority 2

- Upgrade the summary cards so they show implementation-ready details, especially rough screens and `visualguide.md` excerpts.
- Add a rough-screens stat and/or preview so the screen plan is visibly part of the handoff.

### Priority 3

- Tune mobile hierarchy and scroll behavior once the stronger review/action structure is in place.
- Validate whether celebration copy still feels satisfying after moving it to post-completion only.

## Validation Plan

- Run browser QA on `2.6 -> 2.7 -> 3.1` with real lesson data on desktop and mobile.
- Measure whether students can explain the first Build slice using only the `2.7` page.
- Check whether back-navigation from `3.1` into prior Design lessons drops after the handoff is strengthened.
- Sample completed `2.7` states and judge whether the saved handoff is implementation-ready without opening five prior lessons.
- Watch for support signals about confusion at the Design-to-Build boundary.

## Narrative Summary

`2.7` is directionally right: it treats the Design wrap-up as a curated handoff instead of another blank form. The problem is that the current experience leans too hard on recap and celebration, and not hard enough on readiness. It tells the student Build is unlocked before it truly asks them to verify anything, summarizes artifact presence better than handoff quality, underplays rough screens, and does not give the transition into `3.1` enough operational guidance.

The next version should stay short, but become more exacting. This lesson should feel like a fast preflight check that proves Build is set up cleanly, shows the specific assets implementation will depend on, and gives the student one clear final decision: yes, the first slice is now ready to build.
