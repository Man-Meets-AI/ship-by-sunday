# Lesson 2.5 UX Critique: Turn Sketches Into Rough Screens

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to move a paying student from idea to shipped v1 over one guided weekend. Each lesson should remove one concrete source of drag, create visible progress, and make the next lesson easier to start.

### Surface goal

Lesson `2.5` should convert low-fidelity sketches into a real set of buildable rough screens inside Figma. This is the moment where the design work stops being abstract and starts becoming an actionable interface plan for the next lessons.

### Business model

This is a paid course product with gated access via Stripe, Clerk, and Convex. The product wins when students feel momentum, trust the sequence, and believe the system is forcing real progress rather than letting them click through performative steps.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `2.4` to `2.5` continuation rate
- `2.5` completion rate
- `2.5` to `2.6` continuation rate
- time from `2.5` page load to first rough-screen creation in Figma
- percentage of students who reach `2.6` with a usable rough-screen set
- support/refund signals around “I do not know what counts as a rough screen”
- percentage of students who complete `2.5` without later showing evidence of actual screens

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: this lesson is supposed to be a real making step, not just a lecture checkpoint.
- Assumption: the lesson video is still pending, because the coach content is created with `createPendingLessonCoach`.
- Assumption: the intended handoff artifact is `rough_screens`, because `2.6` explicitly depends on it.
- Confidence: high on the completion and shell-structure issues because they are explicit in the lesson config, renderer, and test coverage; medium on exact student severity because this pass did not include live browser QA.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The lesson can be completed without building any rough screens.

User cannot trust that `2.5` means “rough screens exist,” causing a fake handoff into `2.6`, because the lesson is a watch-only shell with no required fields, no objective tasks, and no completion issues. The tests explicitly lock in that empty-state contract, and the footer CTA can complete the lesson without any saved evidence.

Impact:

- Students can unlock `2.6` without producing the artifact the course claims they made.
- `2.6` is forced to assume rough screens exist when the system never verifies that.
- The product teaches symbolic progression at the exact point where students need enforced making momentum.

### 2. The core artifact is invisible on the page.

User cannot tell what “3–5 Rough Screens” should look like when done, causing uncertainty and low-quality output, because the watch shell only shows the title, intro, video, transcript, resources, and footer. It does not expose pinned artifacts, examples, a rough-screen checklist, or any preview of what a valid output contains.

Impact:

- Learners do not get a concrete definition of “rough enough but buildable.”
- Nervous students may over-design; confident students may under-produce.
- The artifact label in the chrome is more specific than the teaching on the page.

### 3. The lesson weakens the bridge from sketches and storyboard into Figma.

User cannot feel the chain from prior lessons to this making step, causing re-interpretation work, because `2.5` depends on `figma_file_ready` and pins `sketch_set` plus `storyboard_image`, but the watch shell does not surface those artifacts. The student is told to turn sketches into screens, yet the actual saved sketches and storyboard are off-screen.

Impact:

- Students have to remember previous outputs instead of being guided by them.
- The design sequence loses its “each step compounds into the next” feeling.
- `2.5` becomes more like a context switch than a continuation.

### 4. The primary action is underdesigned for an external-tool lesson.

User cannot see one dominant next move, causing friction in the Figma handoff, because the watch shell provides no strong “open Figma now” action, no explicit “keep your sketches beside you” framing, and no structured work surface for an external-tool task. The richer `external_workspace` workbench exists, but watch lessons bypass it entirely.

Impact:

- The lesson feels passive when it should feel operational.
- Students can watch, skim, and click complete without making the tool switch.
- The product spends a full lesson on rough screens without making the external work session feel intentional.

### 5. The first screen-building lesson opens with a placeholder instead of a demonstration.

User cannot calibrate the speed and fidelity bar for rough screens, causing hesitation or incorrect output, because the most important teaching surface is the watch video and the coach video is currently pending. This specific lesson needs show-don't-tell more than the surrounding planning lessons.

Impact:

- First-time or design-shy students do not get a confidence transfer.
- The page feels incomplete at the moment the user most needs a model to copy.
- Students are more likely to stall, skip, or make the wrong level of fidelity.

### 6. The completion state does not reinforce what should now exist in Figma.

User does not leave the lesson with a crisp sense of done, causing a soft transition into `2.6`, because the completion banner is generic and the artifact lock language does not restate the observable outcome. It celebrates system state instead of concrete design state.

Impact:

- Students may leave unsure whether they have enough screens, enough detail, or the right sequence.
- `2.6` inherits avoidable ambiguity around what it is reviewing.
- The course loses some of its authority as a guided build system.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Add a real rough-screen proof gate before completion.

What to change:

- Add a compact required confirmation or evidence block for `2.5`.
- Minimum viable version:
  - `I built 3–5 rough screens in Figma`
  - `The screens follow my saved sketch/storyboard flow`
  - `The screens include enough labels and structure to build from`
- Better version:
  - require one text field summarizing which 3–5 screens were made
  - or require 1–3 screenshots/uploaded captures of the roughs

Why it solves the user problem:

- It makes the `rough_screens` artifact real instead of symbolic.
- It forces the student to cross the threshold from watching to making.

How it supports business growth:

- Better quality at `2.5` should improve `2.6` usefulness and downstream build confidence.
- It protects trust that course progress reflects actual work completed.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Adds friction to a lesson designed to feel fast.
- Screenshot uploads are stronger evidence but heavier than lightweight confirmations.

How to validate:

- Compare `2.5` to `2.6` continuation quality before and after the gate.
- Review whether students entering `2.6` now consistently have actual rough screens.

### 2. Surface the pinned artifacts directly in the watch shell.

What to change:

- Add a compact “Use these as your source” section in the watch layout for:
  - `sketch_set`
  - `storyboard_image`
- Show the saved artifacts above the resources section, not hidden behind another route or prior memory.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It keeps the student anchored to the work they already did.
- It turns the transition into “translate this” instead of “start over.”

How it supports business growth:

- Better continuity increases perceived quality and reduces rework.
- Students are more likely to produce cleaner screens faster.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much artifact detail could clutter a watch lesson.
- The surfaced previews need to stay concise and visual-first.

How to validate:

- QA whether a student can explain what inputs they are using after one glance.
- Review whether rough-screen outputs align more consistently with the storyboard sequence.

### 3. Redesign the watch shell for external-tool execution, not just viewing.

What to change:

- Add one dominant action block near the top:
  - `Open Figma`
  - `Keep your sketches/storyboard visible`
  - `Build these 3–5 key screens only`
- Consider allowing watch lessons with `external_workspace` to reuse a slimmed-down version of the external workbench rather than the generic watch shell.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It gives the page an operational posture.
- It makes the tool switch feel like part of the lesson, not a side effect.

How it supports business growth:

- Students move into real work faster.
- The product feels more intentionally designed around making, not just content consumption.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Reusing workbench patterns for watch lessons may add implementation complexity.
- Too much chrome could dilute the simplicity of the current watch layout.

How to validate:

- Time the delay between page load and first Figma action in QA sessions.
- Compare completion quality between the passive watch shell and a more action-oriented version.

### 4. Replace placeholder-first teaching with a practical fallback until media exists.

What to change:

- If the video is not attached, replace the generic placeholder with a structured fallback that teaches:
  - what screens to make first
  - what fidelity level is enough
  - common overbuilding mistakes
  - what done looks like before `2.6`
- Attach a short real walkthrough clip as soon as possible.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It reduces the “broken lesson” feeling.
- It gives design-anxious students a workable standard to follow.

How it supports business growth:

- Better activation through Design reduces drop-off in the middle of the course.
- A more complete feeling lesson improves trust in the paid product.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Text fallback will still be weaker than video for this lesson type.
- A dense fallback can make a quick making lesson feel heavier.

How to validate:

- Ask QA users whether they can confidently start rough screens with the fallback alone.
- Compare completion and next-step confidence once real media is attached.

### 5. Define the artifact in student language and show it in the completion state.

What to change:

- Supplement `3–5 Rough Screens` with outcome language such as:
  - `A first-pass Figma screen set ready for visual direction`
  - `3–5 buildable screens in sequence`
- In the completion banner, restate what should now exist:
  - `You now have a rough screen set in Figma. Next, choose a visual direction for these screens.`

Why it solves the user problem:

- It makes “done” observable and legible.
- It strengthens the handoff to `2.6`.

How it supports business growth:

- Clearer outputs reduce support questions and keep momentum high.
- Students feel a stronger sense of real progress after each lesson.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Copy alone will not fix empty completion if no gate is added.
- The wording needs to stay concrete and not sound like internal product language.

How to validate:

- QA whether students can describe the finished artifact in one sentence.
- Review whether fewer students arrive in `2.6` still unsure if they are ready.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

- The main decision is whether `2.5` should remain a pure watch lesson or become a lightweight evidence-capturing lesson. For this surface, the current watch-only contract is too weak because the next lesson depends on a real artifact.
- The thinnest acceptable fix is a required readiness/evidence check plus pinned-artifact visibility. That preserves speed while making completion mean something.
- The better long-term fix is to treat external-tool watch lessons as a separate pattern: video plus source artifacts plus explicit action steps plus lightweight evidence capture.
- If scope is tight, prioritize behavioral integrity over polish. A real completion gate matters more than a better empty-state card.

## Priority Plan

### Priority 1

Make `2.5` completion require evidence or explicit confirmation that rough screens exist.

### Priority 2

Surface `sketch_set` and `storyboard_image` directly in the watch shell so the student builds from visible source material.

### Priority 3

Add an action-oriented external-tool block at the top of the lesson so the tool switch is unmistakable.

### Priority 4

Improve the missing-video fallback and attach real lesson media as soon as available.

### Priority 5

Tighten the output and completion copy so the artifact and next step are clearer in student language.

## Validation Plan

- Run browser QA on the `2.4 -> 2.5 -> 2.6` path with one user who is comfortable in Figma and one who is not.
- Track whether `2.5` can still be completed with zero real work after the fix.
- Inspect completed `2.5` lesson states to verify the stored artifact now reflects actual rough-screen progress.
- Measure whether more users arrive in `2.6` with a usable screen sequence instead of ambiguity.
- Review support signals for “what counts as a rough screen?” and “am I done yet?”

## Narrative Summary

Lesson `2.5` is supposed to be the first real screen-making step in the Design block, but the current UX treats it like a passive watch checkpoint. The product says the student should build `3–5 Rough Screens`, yet the system validates nothing, shows almost none of the upstream source material, and opens with a missing-video placeholder. That makes the lesson easy to clear and easy to misunderstand.

The strongest move here is not a cosmetic redesign. It is to restore behavioral integrity. Make completion mean that rough screens actually exist, keep the sketches and storyboard visible on the page, and make the Figma transition feel like the core action of the lesson. Once that is true, this lesson can do its real job: turn design work from vague intention into a buildable screen set that `2.6` can refine instead of inventing from scratch.
