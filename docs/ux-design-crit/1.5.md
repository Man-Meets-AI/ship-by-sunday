# Lesson 1.5 UX Critique: Map the Core Flow

## Outcome Context

### Product goal

`Ship by Sunday` needs to move a paying student from a fuzzy app idea to a shipped v1 over one guided weekend. The product works when each lesson produces a concrete artifact, narrows the next decision, and keeps momentum high enough that students do not stall out in planning.

### Surface goal

Lesson `1.5` needs to turn the chosen user and promised outcome into one simple, legible core flow that the student can use in `1.6` to cut scope. This screen should help the student externalize the product sequence quickly, not just collect any image file.

### Business model

This is a paid digital course sold through Stripe with access delivered through Clerk and Convex. Revenue quality depends on students activating quickly after purchase, progressing through the weekend sprint, feeling that each lesson earns its place, and not losing trust because the instruction is vague or incomplete.

### Business metrics that matter here

- `1.4` to `1.5` progression rate
- `1.5` completion rate
- `1.5` to `1.6` continuation rate
- Share of students who upload a replacement image after the first try
- Failed completion attempts on `1.5`
- Time from page load to first upload
- Support or refund signals around "what counts as a core flow?" or "is this good enough?"
- Downstream quality of `1.6` scope cuts and `2.1` design decisions

### Assumptions and confidence

- Assumption: students arrive with a defined user and outcome, but many still have too much solution sprawl in their head.
- Assumption: this lesson is supposed to force simplification before design work starts, so clarity matters more than depth.
- Assumption: the critique is grounded in the current lesson contract and shared lesson shell behavior, not live student recordings or analytics.
- Confidence: high on the structural critique because the lesson contract, video state, upload behavior, and completion logic are explicit in code; medium on exact severity because this pass did not include a live browser session.

## Problems I Notice

### 1. The lesson says "watch first," but the primary teaching surface is currently a placeholder state.

User cannot learn what a good core flow looks like before going off-screen, causing uncertainty and lower artifact quality, because `1.5` uses `createPendingLessonCoach(...)` and `LessonVideoSurface` renders a generic placeholder when no real video is attached.

Impact:

- The page asks for a walkthrough-dependent action without delivering the walkthrough.
- Students have to infer the standard from sparse copy and optional examples elsewhere.
- This weakens trust in the lesson at a moment where the product should feel highly guided.

### 2. The off-screen paper step has almost no pre-draw scaffold before the student leaves the page.

User cannot confidently start mapping the flow, causing hesitation or overcomplication, because the main action card only repeats "5-7 steps" guidance and does not provide a simple sequence template, a definition of the value moment, or a quick self-check before the student steps away from the app.

Impact:

- Students may default to feature inventory instead of user journey.
- The flow can sprawl because there is no lightweight structure to hold onto.
- More cognitive work is pushed onto the student right before they disengage from the product surface.

### 3. The chosen user and promised outcome are not active enough inside the main action path.

User cannot keep the drawing anchored to the exact promise they are making, causing generic or bloated flow maps, because the relevant artifacts only appear in a collapsed `Need context?` disclosure below the upload area instead of shaping the drawing task up front.

Impact:

- Students can draw a category-level system instead of a flow for their chosen user.
- The product loses the compounding effect of `1.2` and `1.3`.
- Downstream scope cutting in `1.6` becomes harder because the artifact is less opinionated.

### 4. Completion is gated by file presence, not by flow legibility.

User can satisfy the system without proving the flow is clear enough to guide the next lesson, causing false completion, because the required artifact is only `core_flow_image` and the validation copy only checks whether one upload exists.

Impact:

- A blurry, overstuffed, or poorly sequenced image can pass.
- The lesson rewards compliance more than instructional quality.
- Students can progress into `1.6` with an artifact that is technically saved but strategically weak.

### 5. The upload state confirms storage, but it does not confirm usefulness or what happens next.

User cannot tell whether their upload is "good enough" or how it will be used in the next lesson, causing trust and momentum loss, because the current upload UI only says the file was saved and shows a preview without any teaching feedback or next-step framing.

Impact:

- Students may second-guess whether they should retake the photo.
- The value of the artifact stays abstract.
- The handoff into scope cutting feels administrative instead of causal.

### 6. The lesson intentionally reduced interface complexity, but it may now under-support the most failure-prone moment.

User can move through the screen quickly, but not always correctly, because the focus-only paper-upload flow removed the older complexity while also stripping away most of the in-product coaching that would help weaker students make a clean flow.

Impact:

- Strong students will probably move faster.
- Weaker students may finish faster with lower-quality output.
- The simplification improves throughput but may reduce instructional yield unless the key coaching is reintroduced in a tighter form.

## Solutions I Recommend

### 1. Add a real no-video fallback for the launch period.

What to change:

- If the lesson video is still pending, replace the generic placeholder with a lesson-specific fallback block inside the `Watch first` card.
- Include:
  - one sentence defining a core flow
  - one good 5-step example
  - one "too big" example
  - one reminder that the goal is the shortest believable sequence to the promised outcome

Why it solves the user problem:

- It restores minimum viable instruction when the canonical teaching asset is not attached.
- It gives the student a usable quality bar before they go off-screen.

How it supports business growth:

- Reduces trust loss during launch while recording/media hosting is still incomplete.
- Keeps lesson quality from depending entirely on missing video inventory.

Trade-offs or risks:

- The fallback should not become a permanent excuse to delay real video attachment.
- Too much fallback content could reintroduce clutter into a lesson that benefits from simplicity.

How to validate:

- Measure completion and continuation rates for lessons that still use pending-video fallback.
- Review a small sample of saved `1.5` artifacts and compare quality before and after the fallback is added.

### 2. Add a compact pre-draw scaffold above the upload step.

What to change:

- Insert a small "Before you step away" card above the upload field with three prompts:
  - `Start:` what the user brings in
  - `Value moment:` when the promise becomes real
  - `End:` what they can now review, send, or use
- Add a visible 5-step sequence template like `input -> generate -> review -> share -> done`.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It gives students a structure to draw against without turning the lesson into a form.
- It reduces the chance that they sketch an architecture instead of a product flow.

How it supports business growth:

- Better `1.5` artifacts improve the quality of `1.6` and later design work.
- Stronger scaffolding increases the course’s perceived sharpness and reduces support load.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Too much structure could make the lesson feel duplicative or over-instrumented.
- Keep the scaffold short enough that the off-screen step still feels fast.

How to validate:

- Review whether saved flow maps more often show a clear start, value moment, and end.
- Ask test users to explain their flow after upload; success is when they can do it in under 20 seconds.

### 3. Move user/outcome context into the active working surface.

What to change:

- Add a compact context strip directly above the `Do this now` card:
  - `User: <saved user statement>`
  - `Promise: <saved outcome statement>`
- Add one instruction line: `Draw only the shortest path that gets this user to this promised result.`

Why it solves the user problem:

- It keeps the artifact anchored to the exact decision made in the prior lessons.
- It makes the drawing exercise feel like a continuation of the CEO block rather than a reset.

How it supports business growth:

- Increases the compounding value of the lesson sequence.
- Better anchoring improves downstream artifact quality and reduces the chance that students feel the course is repetitive or disconnected.

Trade-offs or risks:

- This duplicates context that technically already exists in pinned artifacts.
- The strip needs to stay concise so it sharpens the task instead of becoming another dense panel.

How to validate:

- Compare saved `1.5` artifacts before and after the change for category drift vs promise-specific flows.
- Watch whether `1.6` scope cuts reference the chosen user/outcome more consistently.

### 4. Add a lightweight quality gate before completion.

What to change:

- Keep the artifact as an uploaded image, but add 2-3 quality checks tied to completion feedback:
  - `The flow has 5-7 steps`
  - `The value moment is obvious`
  - `Someone else could follow this sequence`
- These can be inferred partly from student confirmation checkboxes or required micro-prompts, not just file existence.

Why it solves the user problem:

- It changes the lesson from "upload anything" to "upload something usable."
- It gives students a clearer definition of done without needing heavy analysis.

How it supports business growth:

- Higher-quality artifacts improve later lesson performance.
- Better completion integrity reduces the risk of students moving forward with weak foundations and blaming the course.

Trade-offs or risks:

- Any extra quality gate adds friction to a deliberately light lesson.
- The checks should stay minimal and judgment-friendly, not become bureaucratic.

How to validate:

- Track failed completion attempts and replacement uploads.
- Review whether the proportion of clearly legible maps improves without a major drop in completion rate.

### 5. Turn the post-upload preview into a handoff moment.

What to change:

- After upload, show a brief next-step note near the preview:
  - `Next lesson: cut everything that does not belong in this core flow.`
- Add a simple retake affordance label like `Blurry or crowded? Replace photo before you continue.`

Why it solves the user problem:

- It tells the student how to evaluate the upload and why it matters.
- It makes the artifact feel like an input to a larger process, not a dead-end submission.

How it supports business growth:

- Better narrative continuity increases progression confidence.
- It reinforces the value of the course as a compounding system rather than isolated tasks.

Trade-offs or risks:

- More post-upload copy can become noise if it is too long.
- The retake path needs to stay lightweight so it encourages improvement without stalling progress.

How to validate:

- Measure whether students replace uploads less often after the preview guidance becomes clearer.
- In QA sessions, ask what lesson `1.6` is for immediately after `1.5`; success is when they can answer correctly from the screen state.

## Trade-offs and Decisions

### Decision 1: keep the lesson simple, but reintroduce just enough structure to protect quality.

The current simplification was directionally correct. The lesson should stay focus-only and artifact-light. The change is not to add a big editor back. The change is to add a better pre-draw scaffold and a clearer quality bar so the simplified experience still teaches.

### Decision 2: treat missing video as a launch blocker for instructional clarity, not as a neutral placeholder.

Because the page explicitly tells the student to watch first, a generic pending state is not good enough. Until the real lesson media is attached, the screen needs lesson-specific fallback teaching.

### Decision 3: preserve the image-upload artifact contract, but make "usable image" the real goal.

The uploaded photo is the right artifact for this lesson. The problem is not the artifact type. The problem is that the system currently validates existence more than usefulness. Add small quality checks instead of changing the artifact format.

### Decision 4: anchor the flow to user and outcome inside the main task area, not only in shell-adjacent context.

This lesson sits between strategy and scope. If the user/outcome framing is not active at the point of action, the artifact loses too much of its decision value.

## Priority Plan

### Must

- Replace the generic pending-video state with a `1.5`-specific fallback teaching block until the real walkthrough is attached.
- Add a compact user/outcome context strip in the active workbench.
- Add a lightweight pre-draw scaffold that defines start, value moment, and end.
- Strengthen completion guidance so the artifact must be legible, not just uploaded.

### Should

- Improve the post-upload preview with replace-photo guidance and a clearer `1.6` handoff.
- Add one explicit example of "too big" vs "core flow" in the main lesson path, not only in examples metadata.

### Could

- Later, use image analysis or structured follow-up prompts to help students self-assess flow quality more intelligently.
- Later, test whether a downloadable one-page worksheet frame improves artifact quality for weaker students.

## Validation Plan

- Run browser QA on `1.4 -> 1.5 -> 1.6` with one entitled account on desktop and mobile.
- Confirm the page still feels fast, but that a student can explain what a core flow is before leaving the screen.
- Measure failed completion attempts and replacement uploads after the quality checks are added.
- Sample saved `1.5` artifacts and score them for legibility, step count, and clarity of value moment.
- Check whether `1.6` artifacts show less scope sprawl after the `1.5` changes land.
- Watch for support signals around "what counts here?" or "is my flow good enough?"

## Narrative Summary

Lesson `1.5` has the right ambition and the right artifact shape. It correctly forces the student out of the product and onto paper, which is a good move for speed and clarity. The problem is that the current experience simplifies the interface more than it simplifies the decision. Students are told to watch a walkthrough that is not attached yet, then asked to leave the screen with only minimal structure, and finally allowed to complete the lesson as soon as any image exists.

The recommendation is not to make this lesson heavier. It is to make it sharper. Give the student a real fallback when the video is missing, anchor the task to the chosen user and promise, provide one tight pre-draw scaffold, and validate that the uploaded image is actually usable for the next lesson. That keeps the lesson fast while making it much more likely that `1.6` starts from a real core flow instead of a technically saved but strategically weak artifact.
