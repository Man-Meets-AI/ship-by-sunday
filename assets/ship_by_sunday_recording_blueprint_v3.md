**SHIP BY SUNDAY**  
**Recording Blueprint**

Curriculum \+ Sherpa scripts for recording the sprint

_Teach \-\> Show \-\> Pause \-\> Rep \-\> Return_

**What this document is for:** Use this on your iPad while recording so every lesson stays clear, practical, and action-first.

**North star:** The student should finish one full rep from idea to shipped app \- not just feel informed.

# **Quick Start for Recording**

**•** Read one lesson at a time. Do not try to memorize the whole sprint.

**•** Record like a guide who has done this before: calm, certain, direct.

**•** Keep every lesson moving toward an artifact.

**•** End every lesson with the same 3-part handoff: Your job now / Timebox / Come back with.

**•** If a tangent does not help the student ship by Sunday, cut it.

**Recording formula:** Frame it \-\> Show it \-\> Command it \-\> Pause it \-\> Return and lock it in.

**Tone target:** I have done this before. Follow me. Do the rep.

**Benchmark:** Ugly but done beats smart but unfinished.

## **The 4 blocks**

**•** CEO Block \- Problem, customer, scope.

**•** Design Block \- Flow, screens, direction.

**•** Build Block \- Stack, agent, product.

**•** Ship Block \- Deploy, share, launch.

# **Overview**

**Teach the student how to use the sprint before they begin.**

This should feel like the first five minutes of a good coach: reset expectations, create urgency, and explain the rhythm. The student should understand that watching without pausing is failure.

**End artifact:** Sprint folder duplicated, templates open, mental model of the 4 blocks.

**Recording note:** Record this straight to camera first. It sets the contract for the whole product.

## **0.1 \- Welcome to Ship by Sunday**

**Runtime:** 4-6 min

**Goal:** Set the contract: this is for completion, not consumption.

**Purpose:** Reset the student's behavior. They need to feel guided, slightly challenged, and clear on what this weekend is actually for.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** Welcome to Ship by Sunday. This is not designed to be watched. It is designed to be completed.

**•** I am going to show you what matters, then I am going to tell you to pause and go do the rep.

**•** By the end of this sprint, you do not need to know everything. You need one finished rep.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** What this sprint is: structured, on-demand, one focused weekend.

**•** What this sprint is not: a binge course, a bootcamp, a passive masterclass.

**•** What they leave with: brief, spec, build, live URL.

**•** The rule: do not skip ahead, do not hoard theory, do not wait to feel ready.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** You on camera.

**•** Show the 4-block staircase graphic.

**•** Flash the 4 end artifacts so the finish line feels real.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not over-credential yourself here. Keep it crisp.

**•** Do not explain every block in depth yet; just orient them.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Good. Now you know the rules. Next, I will show you the full path so nothing feels mysterious.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Create a sprint folder and duplicate the provided templates / worksheets.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

# **Block 01 \- The CEO Block**

**Problem. Customer. Scope.**

This block is about making the right thing before you try to make the thing right. Merge strategy, light research, and product judgment into one clean artifact: a one-page brief.

**End artifact:** Worksheet 01 \- One-Page Product Brief.

**Recording note:** This block should feel like product leadership in compressed form: decisive, concrete, anti-bloat.

## **1.1 \- Start With the Problem**

**Runtime:** 6-8 min

**Goal:** Get them out of feature-land and into pain-land.

**Purpose:** Strong apps start with an annoying, expensive, or emotionally charged problem. Novelty is weaker than pain.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** Do not start with "I want to build an AI app." Start with "someone has this painful problem."

**•** Pain creates urgency. Urgency creates value.

**•** You are hunting for friction, not cleverness.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Contrast weak starts with stronger problem statements.

**•** Explain why personal pain is often the best starting point.

**•** Give 2-3 examples from your own life or products.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Whiteboard or notepad with 3 weak ideas and 3 stronger problem statements.

**•** Show your own note-taking pattern if you have one.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not let this become startup theory.

**•** Do not drift into TAM or market size yet.

### **Suggested close**

**•** You now have raw material. Next, we are going to choose one user, because vague users create vague products.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Write down 3 annoying problems from your life or work that would actually be worth solving, and name who has each one.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

**•** Come back with: Three candidate problems.

## **1.2 \- Pick One User**

**Runtime:** 5-7 min

**Goal:** Force specificity and constraint.

**Purpose:** The student needs one user in one situation. Broad audiences kill speed.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** Not everyone. Not the whole market. One real user type in one real moment.

**•** Specificity is not limiting; it is what lets you build something coherent fast.

**•** A narrow user is easier to design for, easier to write for, and easier to test.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Use the sentence: This app is for \_\_\_ who need \_\_\_ without \_\_\_.

**•** Show a bad target and tighten it to something usable.

**•** Explain the difference between a segment and a situation.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Fill the user sentence live on screen.

**•** If possible, show two variants and explain why one is sharper.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not open up multiple personas.

**•** Do not let them stack three user groups into one app.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Good. Now we know who this is for. Next, define what changes for them if this works.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Complete the sentence "This app is for..." and lock one user.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

**•** Come back with: One clear user statement.

## **1.3 \- Define the Outcome**

**Runtime:** 5-6 min

**Goal:** Translate features into transformation.

**Purpose:** Students need to define the promised outcome so the product can be judged by a result, not a pile of features.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** People buy outcomes, not tools.

**•** Your app is a machine for creating one better future state.

**•** If you cannot say the result simply, the product is still foggy.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Write one sentence that starts with: After using this, the user can...

**•** Give weak feature-heavy examples, then rewrite them into outcomes.

**•** Tie this outcome to what will be shown in the demo later.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Show the before/after of weak vs strong promise lines on screen.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not overpromise money or business results.

**•** Keep the outcome grounded and demonstrable.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Now we have the problem, the user, and the outcome. Next, we do just enough research to avoid building blind.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Write one user-facing outcome statement.

**•** Timebox: 5 minutes

**•** Come back with: One clear promised outcome.

## **1.4 \- Light Research, Not a Thesis**

**Runtime:** 8-10 min

**Goal:** Gather signal without disappearing into research hell.

**Purpose:** Students need pattern awareness, not a dissertation.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** You are not writing a market report. You are trying to understand how this gets solved today.

**•** Look for existing solutions, user complaints, and repeated expectations.

**•** Borrow patterns. Avoid blind reinvention.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Show quick scans of Google, app stores, Reddit, or product sites.

**•** Capture: what they do well, what they do poorly, what users seem to care about.

**•** Keep the note format brutally simple.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Screen-share a fast research pass and capture 3 short notes.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not let this become competitor paralysis.

**•** No long rants about strategy books.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Good. You have enough signal. Now turn the product into a simple journey.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Find 2-3 existing solutions and write down what they do well, badly, and what users seem to care about.

**•** Timebox: 20 minutes

**•** Come back with: Three short research notes.

## **1.5 \- Map the Core Flow**

**Runtime:** 7-9 min

**Goal:** Turn the app into a 5-7 step user journey.

**Purpose:** This is where the product becomes navigable. If they cannot map it, they cannot design or build it cleanly.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** If you cannot explain the flow in 5 to 7 steps, it is probably too muddy.

**•** Words and arrows are enough. This is a functional map, not a mural.

**•** Beginning, middle, end.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** List actors if needed, but keep it light.

**•** Write the ending first if that helps.

**•** Focus on the critical moment where the product actually delivers value.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Draw the flow live with simple boxes and arrows.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not add edge cases too early.

**•** Do not let them map three different products at once.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Now we know the path. Last step: cut scope until this can actually ship.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Map the core flow in 5-7 steps using only words and arrows.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

**•** Come back with: A simple flow map.

## **1.6 \- Kill Scope**

**Runtime:** 6-8 min

**Goal:** Prevent weekend death by feature creep.

**Purpose:** The key behavior here is subtraction.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** A smaller shipped app beats a larger imaginary one.

**•** Version one is not supposed to impress everyone. It is supposed to work.

**•** What has to exist by Sunday? Everything else is later.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Split features into: must-have, nice-to-have, definitely-not-now.

**•** Define what good enough for Sunday means.

**•** Cut at least three things live.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Show the worksheet being tightened in real time.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not let them hide bloat behind future-proofing.

**•** Keep the win condition explicit.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Great. You now have a brief. Next, we turn the idea into something concrete enough to build.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Fill out the MVP section and cut at least 3 things.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

**•** Come back with: A tighter MVP scope.

## **1.7 \- CEO Block Wrap-Up**

**Runtime:** 3-5 min

**Goal:** Lock the brief so the next block has real inputs.

**Purpose:** This is the handoff from thinking to form.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** By now, somebody else should be able to understand what you are building in under 60 seconds.

**•** If the brief is foggy, fix it now. It does not get cheaper later.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Review the completed worksheet quickly.

**•** Point to the problem, user, outcome, flow, and scope.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Show the final one-page brief.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not introduce new concepts here.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Nice. The app now exists on paper. In the next block, we make it visible.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Complete Worksheet 01 fully.

**•** Timebox: 15 minutes

**•** Come back with: A finished one-page product brief.

# **Block 02 \- The Design Block**

**Flow. Screens. Direction.**

This block converts an abstract idea into something AI can actually help build. It should feel fast, practical, and anti-perfectionist.

**End artifact:** Worksheet 02 \- Build-Ready Design Spec.

**Recording note:** This is where you act like a creative director with a stopwatch: enough taste to guide, enough discipline to move.

## **2.1 \- Find References First**

**Runtime:** 6-8 min

**Goal:** Stop them from inventing from a blank brain.

**Purpose:** Use reference gathering to reduce randomness and raise taste quickly.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** Do not design from a blank brain.

**•** Steal structure, not identity.

**•** References compress years of pattern exposure into minutes.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Show where you actually look: Mobbin, Spotted in Prod, SaaS sites, apps, whatever you really use.

**•** Capture both UX flow and visual tone.

**•** Tell them what to notice: layout, hierarchy, motion, trust cues.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Screen-share a fast reference hunt and drag 3-5 screenshots into one board.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not let them scroll forever.

**•** Do not let this become moodboard cosplay.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Good. You have raw visual ingredients. Now sketch the product before software gets involved.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Gather 3-5 references close to your desired flow or look.

**•** Timebox: 10 minutes

**•** Come back with: A mini inspiration board.

## **2.2 \- Sketch Before Software**

**Runtime:** 7-9 min

**Goal:** Get them using paper to remove ambiguity fast.

**Purpose:** Sketching is speed. It forces decisions before the build starts lying to you.

### **Suggested opening lines**

**•** Ugly sketches are fine. Vague prompts are not.

**•** You are not making art. You are making decisions.

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Use one core flow and sketch only the important screens.

**•** If stuck, use a 6-up exercise and make six ugly variants fast.

### **On screen / screen share**

**•** Overhead camera or a quick paper demo. Keep it raw.

### **Watchouts**

**•** Do not over-teach sketching theory.

**•** Do not apologize for roughness.

### **Suggested close**

**•** Perfect. Now we string these moments into a sequence.

### **Pause prompt**

**•** Your job now: Sketch the core screens of your app on paper.

**•** Timebox: 15 minutes

**•** Come back with: Rough paper sketches of the main screens.

# **Block 03 \- The Build Block**

**Stack. Agent. Product.**

This block should feel controlled, practical, and anti-chaos. The goal is not to tour every tool; the goal is to get the core flow working locally.

**End artifact:** Worksheet 03 \- Build Plan \+ Agent Brief.

**Recording note:** Keep the student on one default path. Reduce branching. Every extra option costs momentum.

# **Block 04 \- The Ship Block**

**Deploy. Share. Launch.**

This block finishes the rep. It should teach the student that shipping is part of building, not a separate emotional event that happens later.

**End artifact:** Worksheet 04 \- Launch Plan \+ Feedback Tracker.

**Recording note:** This is where you become more demanding: push them to publish, send, and learn.

## **4.1 \- Ship the Core, Not the Fantasy**

**Runtime:** 5-6 min

**Purpose:** Most students stall here because they compare the current product to the dream product.

**•** Ugly and live beats beautiful and trapped.

**•** Version one is supposed to prove the core, not satisfy every fantasy.

**•** The question is simple: does the main promise work?

### **Beats to cover**

**•** Define the shipping checklist.

**•** Separate critical from optional.

**•** Show what good enough to ship looks like.

### **On screen / screen share**

# **Appendix \- Production Notes**

## **Recording tone**

**•** Calm, direct, and slightly demanding.

**•** Short sentences beat long explanations.

**•** Sound like a guide who has done this before.

## **Shot mix**

**•** Use direct-to-camera for framing, mindset, and closes.

**•** Use screen share for examples, workflow, and tool demos.

**•** Use overhead or iPad shots only when sketching actually benefits from it.

## **Editing rule**

**•** Cut dead air aggressively.

**•** Leave a short beat right before every pause prompt.

**•** Use on-screen text for Your job now / Timebox / Come back with.

## **What to avoid**

**•** Do not over-teach tools.

**•** Do not promise business outcomes you cannot back up.

**•** Do not let tangents replace reps.
